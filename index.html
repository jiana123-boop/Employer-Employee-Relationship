SECTION 01

Foundations

Basic concepts and chapter aims

01

Objectives of the Chapter

PDF p. 2
  • Understand the significance of the employer–employee relationship.
  • Explore different tests of the employer–employee relationship.
  • Recognize employee categories, working conditions, and benefits.

Jurisprudence

PDF p. 3
  • From the Latin Jurisprudencia: “jure” or “juris” means law, and “prudentia” means skill or knowledge.
  • Means knowledge or skill of law; the philosophy or theory of law.
  • Concerns what the law is and what it ought to be.
  • “Ignorance of the law excuses no one.”

Employer–Employee Relationship (EER)

PDF p. 4
  • A legal link between employers and employees that creates reciprocal rights and obligations.
  • Based on mutual reliance: employers depend on employees to perform work or services, and employees depend on employers for remuneration.
SECTION 02

Tests of Employment

Determining whether an employer–employee relationship exists

02

Purpose of the Tests

PDF p. 5
  • Used when a complainant alleges an employment relationship but a company denies it.
  • Disputes arise from misclassification (regular or non-regular), incorrect contracts, or labor-law violations.

Four-Fold Test — Selection & Engagement

PDF p. 6
  • Examines hiring and recruitment by the employer.
  • Important to examine where third parties are involved in contracting or subcontracting.

Four-Fold Test — Payment of Wages

PDF p. 6
  • Payslips are proof of wage payment by the employer.
  • The employer’s records must also show the existence of the employee to establish the EER.

Four-Fold Test — Power of Dismissal

PDF p. 6
  • The module describes employer power to separate employees for authorized causes, with separation pay given to affected employees.

Four-Fold Test — Control

PDF p. 6
  • Examines active supervision and control of an individual performing the work or service.
  • Includes orders, directives, or instructions, dictating how tasks are performed, and even specifying tools and instruments to be used.

Economic Reality Test

PDF p. 7
  • Examines the economic realities within an activity or between the parties.
  • The module describes using this test where there is no written contract or agreement on which to base the relationship.

SECTION 04

Categories of Employees

Descriptions reproduced from the chapter’s organization

04

Regular Employment — Article 280

PDF p. 10
  • An employee is engaged to perform activities usually necessary or desirable to the ordinary business or trade of the employer.

Regular/Casual Employee

PDF p. 10
  • The focus is on the necessity or desirability of the activity performed by the employee.

Probationary Employee

PDF p. 10
  • May become a regular employee after passing or qualifying for regular employment during the probationary period.
  • A probationary employee is not yet a regular employee while under probation (Article 281, LCP).

Seasonal Employee

PDF p. 11
  • Performs seasonal work or services, with employment for the duration of the season.

Independent Contractors

PDF p. 12
  • Offer professional services to clients in exchange for professional fees.
  • Enter into a contract for services rather than an employment contract; the module lists retainer agreements and examples such as lawyers, doctors, engineers, and consultants.

Fixed-Term Employment

PDF p. 13
  • The fixed term is knowingly and voluntarily agreed on, without force, duress, improper pressure, or circumstances that invalidate consent.
  • Employer and employee deal with each other on more or less equal terms, with no moral dominance exercised by either party.
